[Repeat Repairs slide - 61%]

Every producing well in Strathcona's heavy oil fields runs on a pump, and

planned, but every visit costs a crew, a truck and lost oil. Today a
manager picks who to send from experience, and a crew picks a fix from

actually found is buried in a job summary.

[Research Question slide]

HASSAAN:

that reduce repeat repairs and costs compared with current practice?


====================================================================
2. SOLUTION - ABDULLAH (1:00 - 1:45)
====================================================================

[Our Solution slide]

ABDULLAH:
Many tools can tell you which well is at risk. WellSimple also PREPARES
THE CREW for the visit, and LEARNS from every note they send back. It is
two connected apps. Managers get a ranked list of which wells to visit
first and what each repair is likely to cost. Crews get a phone app that
puts what past crews found in their hands before they arrive. Behind it
are a risk model, a searchable memory of nearly FOURTEEN THOUSAND real
crew notes, and a randomized pilot to measure the savings. It all runs on
DATABRICKS, and nothing in it is AI-generated: every note you'll see is a
real crew's own words. Hassaan will start with where risk comes from.


====================================================================
3. RISK AND SENSORS - HASSAAN (1:45 - 3:00)
====================================================================

[Data sources slide]

HASSAAN:
We use three sources, and each answers a different question. SENSORS tell
us WHEN a well needs attention. REPAIR HISTORY is the STRONGEST risk
signal. CREW NOTES tell us WHAT you'll find and HOW LONG a fix will last.

[Sensor Data slide]

HASSAAN:
These wells use progressing cavity pumps: a motor at the surface turns a
rod string that drives a pump downhole. We track nine daily readings as
trends, and we added features from the pump's physics, like how fast
efficiency is dropping, and a FATIGUE INDEX: loaded pump revolutions since
the last repair. Sensors lift accuracy from 0.76 to 0.82, and the top five
percent of wells we flag needed a repair FORTY-NINE PERCENT of the time,
against twelve percent overall. From sensors alone we can predict the
likely failing part. And we didn't take textbook warning signs on faith.
We checked them against what crews actually found. GAS INTERFERENCE held
up, THREE AND A HALF TIMES. Others didn't, so the app shows them as
signals, not causes.

4. REPAIRS AND CREW NOTES - UZAIR (3:00 - 4:30)
====================================================================

[Repair History slide]

UZAIR:
Repair history is the STRONGEST single signal, and the reason SURPRISED
us. Risk is HIGHEST RIGHT AFTER A FIX: about TEN TIMES higher in the first
week than six to twelve months later. And that holds even comparing each
well ONLY WITH ITSELF, so it isn't just bad wells failing first. That
means SCHEDULED REPLACEMENT WOULDN'T PAY, because every new install starts
in its riskiest weeks. The money is in getting each repair RIGHT.
It also sizes the problem: about THIRTEEN POINT NINE MILLION DOLLARS A
YEAR goes to repairs within thirty days of a previous one, and the worst
TEN PERCENT of wells take THIRTY-FIVE PERCENT of the jobs. Combine each
well's risk with an average repair cost of about seventeen and a half
thousand dollars plus lost oil, and you get its EXPECTED LOSS. That's what
ranks the manager's list.

[Crew Notes slide]

UZAIR:
Crew notes do something sensors CAN'T: they're the BEST PREDICTOR of how
long a repair will last, 0.69 versus 0.58 from sensors. For every well we
show REAL PAST JOBS, what they cost and how long they held. NOTHING IS
GENERATED, and the matching works even across fields, so it isn't just
copying one area's writing style. And it's a CLOSED LOOP: every note a
crew submits becomes a past job the NEXT CREW can find.
Abdullah will show you why you can trust it.

Q&A - SHORT ANSWERS (about 15 seconds each, then stop)
====================================================================

Never name or criticize other teams. Say what makes ours different.

"How do you know it isn't overfitting?"
  Trained on earlier data, left a thirty-day gap, tested only on months
  it never saw. Beats each well's own history (0.72) and the field
  average (0.68), and it's calibrated: when it says 42%, 43% happen.

"Another team got higher precision in their top wells."
  Different targets, so not a direct comparison. Ours predicts any repair
  visit in 30 days, including scheduled work, across all 1,336 wells,
  and it beats each well's own history and is calibrated.

"Isn't the early-failure spike just planned follow-ups?"
  Some of it may be. But the pattern holds within the same well (shape
  0.71). Separating planned from unplanned visits is our first refinement.

"Why not recommend a fix, like a new rod string?"
  Our most repair-prone wells sit closest to constant risk, which points
  to redesign rather than another repair, and WellSimple puts exactly
  those wells in front of engineers. We didn't claim a causal saving from
  history; that's why we built a randomized pilot.

"Where does $13.9M come from? And $1.7M to $9.1M?"
  Field-estimated cost of repair jobs that started within thirty days of
  a previous repair on the same well, averaged over the job log. Add
  $2.6M of lost oil, times a 10% to 55% reduction (55% is a published
  root-cause program on these pumps). Scenarios, which is why we built
  the pilot.

"What about faster dispatch?"
  In the failure log, 94% of failures already have a repair job dated on
  or before the failure date, so we found little queue to shorten.
  Downtime codes may capture waiting the log doesn't - worth a pilot.

"Did you use Canary?"
  We built on Databricks instead: pipeline job, Delta tables and Vector
  Search, with the live app fed directly from it.

"How is your search different from a plain vector search?"
  Every result carries its outcome (cost, how long it lasted), sensors
  can find notes before anyone writes one, it matches exact search 99% of
  the time, and it sits inside a risk model and a crew loop.

"Why no AI language model?"
  In the field, trust matters. Every note is a real crew's words, so
  nothing can be made up. We also tested a neural text model; the simpler
  method did better on predicting how long repairs last.

"What does 0.69 concordance mean?"
  Pick two repairs: 69% of the time it correctly says which will last
  longer. A coin flip is 50%.

"Can the pilot detect a small improvement?"
  Not reliably: at 183 wells per group it catches a 50% cut 96% of the
  time, but a 20% cut only about a quarter of the time. Small effects
  need more wells or longer.

"Your failure data is short?"
  Labeled failures cover about 22 months, so component predictions use
  that window. The job log covers almost six years, which the repair
  timing and repeat analysis use.

"What did you build the charts with?"
  Chart.js for the charts and Leaflet with OpenStreetMap for the map.

full method is in our repo." Then stop.
